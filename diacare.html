DiaCare
From plans to daily actions

Connecting meal planning, food logging, and grocery preparation in one application.

Developed by Tarik Alansari

Technologies Flutter · Dart · Firebase · Cloud Functions

OverviewApproachImplementationScope & next steps

01Project overview

I developed DiaCare to connect the steps between planning a meal and recording what someone actually ate. The application brings together personal meal plans, food and medication logs, grocery preparation, and messaging with a care professional.

My focus in this report is the application engineering behind those workflows: keeping shared records consistent, making food suggestions reviewable, and handling interrupted requests. The screens below show the implemented meal-plan and grocery interfaces, rather than generated product mockups.

02How I built it

  1. Connect planning and logging. Build Flutter screens for browsing meals, reviewing nutrition details, logging a planned meal, and swapping a choice. Manual logging and the planner use a shared meal-log repository.
  2. Carry the plan into preparation. Connect the meal plan to a grocery list organised by food category, with an estimated total shown separately from a confirmed purchase price.
  3. Keep photo suggestions editable. Ask for consent before sending a food photo for analysis, then let the user confirm foods and portions before logging. Search and manual entry provide alternative paths.

Engineering decisions

A shared repository gives the planner and manual log one source of truth. Meal entries retain base nutrient values and a portion multiplier so repeated portion edits do not keep scaling already-adjusted values. Missing optional nutrient values remain distinct from zero.

Photo analysis uses a shared deadline across its asynchronous stages and a cancellation mechanism that lets the interface stop waiting for an in-flight request. Cancellation does not imply that a remote request has stopped. Account-boundary handling separates user data from the small set of device preferences that may persist.

03Implementation & verification

The implemented flow connects meal choices to logging and grocery preparation. The screenshots show example meals, nutrition values, and a grocery estimate; these values illustrate the interface rather than a clinical outcome or a recommended diet.

Repository tests cover photo cancellation, late errors after cancellation, analysis timeouts, a shared deadline across stages, portion conversions, and navigation from the meal plan to groceries. These checks address implementation behaviour; they do not establish food-recognition accuracy, usability, or health benefits.

04Scope & next steps

This case study describes the implementation in the project repository. Available product features may vary by release or enrolment. Food suggestions and portion estimates require user review, and catalogue prices are estimates. No usage, clinical-effectiveness, or diagnostic-performance result is claimed here.

Takeaway & next step. The difficult part is making a plan, a log, and a grocery list behave as one coherent experience when users edit entries or a request fails. Next, I would evaluate those complete tasks with users and measure completion and recovery behaviour before making usability claims.
